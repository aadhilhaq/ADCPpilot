Your first run

Open the finished example first, then dock one short peptide of your own with small settings. Both take minutes, and between them they show every part of the page.

The example run

A finished run ships with the package. Add it to your runs folder with:

adcp-dock example

It is MDM2 (PDB entry 1YCR, chain A) with three peptides that are known to bind it: p53 ETFSDLWKLLPEN, PMI TSFAEYWNLLS and the 12/1 core RFMDYWEGL. All three were docked and rescored with MM-GBSA. Reload the page and it appears in Runs.

Press open. The ranked table appears below the list, and view on any row opens that peptide's poses in the structure viewer. Read Reading the results beside it: the table has more columns than a first look suggests, and several of them carry warnings about how far they can be pushed.

The example is also a lesson in settings. It ran at 25 replicas by 2,000,000 steps, which is below every published setting, because it ships inside a Python package and had to be small. Its ranking happens to agree with what is known about these three peptides. Do not read that as evidence that 25 replicas is enough.

One of your own

  1. Load a receptor. Type a PDB id and press Fetch from RCSB, or give a path on the cluster, or upload a file from your own computer. The line below reports the atom count and the chains. Tick the chain you want; leaving them all unticked keeps them all.
  2. Name the site. Type the residues a binder must touch, as A:54, A:62, A:67, or click them in the structure or in the sequence strip. Press Check site: it reports the box, its centre and its size, and tells you if the box reaches a chain you did not load.
  3. Look at the cage. Show the docking box draws it. ADCP searches inside it and nowhere else, so check that it covers the groove and not half the protein.
  4. Add one peptide. Press + Add peptide and type a sequence of 5 to 30 residues in one-letter code. The row counts the residues and says at once if something is wrong with it.
  5. Keep the search small for this first run. 25 replicas and 500,000 steps finish in minutes and prove the path works. They are not a result.
  6. Untick Rescore with MM-GBSA for now. It is on by default, it roughly doubles the run, and the point here is to see the whole thing finish.
  7. Press Estimate & fill. It works out how long the run should take and fills in the cores and the walltime, with a margin.
  8. Name the run and choose a folder. On a cluster the folder must be one the compute nodes can see, such as your scratch directory. Then Submit to the queue.

The run appears at the top of the Runs list with a dot that goes amber while it waits, pulsing green while it works, and green when it is done. Open it when it finishes.

Then the real one

With the path proven, raise the search to something defensible. ADCP's own guidance is 100 to 300 replicas and 1 to 3 million Monte Carlo steps per amino acid, so a 10-mer wants 10,000,000 to 30,000,000 steps. Docking settings gives the numbers and where they come from.

Turn MM-GBSA on for a run you intend to rank. The docking score orders the poses of one peptide well, and it is a poor way to compare different peptides, which is usually the question a screen is asking.

Book the walltime generously. A job the scheduler stops at its limit has produced nothing and has already spent its time in the queue. Clusters charge for the time used, not the time booked.

If something does not work, the run's own logs are the place to look, and When something goes wrong lists the messages you are most likely to meet and what each one means.
