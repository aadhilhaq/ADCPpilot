Histidines at the site

A histidine is the one residue whose protonation is genuinely in doubt at physiological pH. Which program places its protons decides what it can do at your site. If one lines your site, this is what to check.

What is decided, and by what

For a histidine there are three answers: a hydrogen on the delta nitrogen, a hydrogen on the epsilon nitrogen, or one on both, which makes the ring positive. That choice decides what the site can do. A neutral histidine can accept a hydrogen bond at its free nitrogen and donate at the other. A doubly protonated one donates at both and carries a charge, which changes both the docking and the rescoring. The two neutral forms are not interchangeable either: they point the hydrogen at opposite sides of the ring.

By default the receptor is protonated with reduce, which places each histidine's protons where the hydrogen bonds around the ring call for them, and may turn the ring over where the file has it the wrong way round. With PROPKA at a pH chosen, pdb2pqr places them and PROPKA predicts each residue's state at the pH you give. There is no per-residue control in either. See protonation.

How to read what happened

Both programs leave the residue named HIS and express the decision by which hydrogens they placed. So the name in receptor/prepared.pdb tells you nothing; the atoms do.

Hydrogens on the ringThe stateWhat it can do
HD1 onlyneutral, delta protonateddonates at ND1, accepts at NE2
HE2 onlyneutral, epsilon protonatedaccepts at ND1, donates at NE2
bothpositivedonates at both, and carries a charge

reduce also writes its reasons. The top of receptor/protonated.pdb holds one USER MOD line per histidine, Asn and Gln, such as A 96 HIS : no HD1, with FLIP where it turned the side chain over.

In the example, MDM2 has two histidines and both are in the site: His73 and His96. The two programs disagree about both. reduce gives each HE2 and no HD1, epsilon protonated, with ND1 free to accept a hydrogen bond; PDB2PQR with PROPKA at pH 7.4, which the shipped example run was made with, gives each HD1, delta protonated, with NE2 free instead. On the dengue envelope protein dimer 7A3R the two disagree on 10 of its 18 histidines.

The MM-GBSA stage names each histidine HID, HIE or HIP before building the Amber topology, so the rescoring uses the decision the docking did rather than a force field default: under reduce from the receptor that was docked into, and under PROPKA from protonating the complex again at the same pH. The interface columns read a residue named HIS as protonated, and read HID and HIE as the neutral forms they are.

Looking at the geometry

Use the pose viewer to look at what the histidine actually does: the contacts are drawn with their measured distances, so you can see whether the peptide is reaching the free nitrogen or the protonated one.

Remember what the contact columns do and do not test. A hydrogen bond here is a nitrogen or oxygen pair within 3.5 Å, measured between heavy atoms. No hydrogen position and no angle is used. So the columns will count a contact at a histidine whichever nitrogen carries the hydrogen, and the geometry is yours to judge.

Two programs, two answers

Checked against the protein structures in the PDB solved by neutron diffraction, where the hydrogens are placed from the data, reduce matched the deposited histidines more often than pdb2pqr (55% against 31% of 1,457 histidines) and turned fewer rings, and fewer Asn and Gln amides, the wrong way. That is why it is the default.

What reduce does not do is pH. It never protonates an Asp or Glu and rarely puts hydrogens on both nitrogens of a histidine, whatever the pH, while PROPKA chooses charges at the pH you set. ADapt protonates with the same reduce, as AGFR does, so a receptor prepared in ADapt and one prepared here with reduce agree.

What to do about it

  1. Keep reduce unless the pH is your question. If your assay runs well away from 7.4, choose PROPKA at that pH.
  2. Read the prepared file at the histidines in your site, and note which nitrogen is free.
  3. Check the poses there. If the best poses all reach the protonated nitrogen, the result depends on a prediction rather than on a measurement.
  4. Run the other program as a second run when a histidine at the site matters. Two runs that agree are worth far more than one that cannot be questioned, and each run records how it was protonated.
