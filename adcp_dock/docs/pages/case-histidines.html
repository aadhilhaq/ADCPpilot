Histidines at the site

A histidine is the one residue whose protonation is genuinely in doubt at physiological pH, and it is decided for you by the pH you set. If one lines your site, this is what to check.

What the pH decides

The receptor is protonated by pdb2pqr, and PROPKA predicts each titratable residue's state at the pH you give. For a histidine there are three answers: a hydrogen on the delta nitrogen, a hydrogen on the epsilon nitrogen, or one on both, which makes the ring positive.

That choice decides what the site can do. A neutral histidine can accept a hydrogen bond at its free nitrogen and donate at the other. A doubly protonated one donates at both and carries a charge, which changes both the docking and the rescoring. The two neutral forms are not interchangeable either: they point the hydrogen at opposite sides of the ring.

There is no per-residue control here. The pH is the whole of the input, and every titratable residue is decided from it. If your assay runs at a pH other than 7.4, that is the number to change, and the run records it.

How to read what happened

pdb2pqr leaves the residue named HIS and expresses its decision by which hydrogens it placed. So the name in receptor/prepared.pdb tells you nothing; the atoms do.

HD1 onlyneutral, delta protonateddonates at ND1, accepts at NE2
HE2 onlyneutral, epsilon protonatedaccepts at ND1, donates at NE2
bothpositivedonates at both, and carries a charge

In the example run, MDM2 has two histidines and both are in the site: His73 and His96. At pH 7.4 both came back with HD1 and no HE2, so both are neutral and delta protonated, with NE2 free to accept a hydrogen bond from a peptide. You can read that straight out of the prepared file.

The MM-GBSA stage renames each histidine from the hydrogens present, to HID, HIE or HIP, before building the Amber topology, so the rescoring uses the same decision the docking did rather than a force field default. The interface columns read a residue named HIS as protonated, and read HID and HIE as the neutral forms they are.

Looking at the geometry

Use ADapt to look at the structure and decide what to keep, and use the pose viewer afterwards to look at what the histidine actually does: the contacts are drawn with their measured distances, so you can see whether the peptide is reaching the free nitrogen or the protonated one.

Remember what the contact columns do and do not test. A hydrogen bond here is a nitrogen or oxygen pair within 3.5 Å, measured between heavy atoms. No hydrogen position and no angle is used. So the columns will count a contact at a histidine whichever nitrogen carries the hydrogen, and the geometry is yours to judge.

Two programs, two answers

ADapt protonates with reduce, as AGFR does, while this interface's preparation uses pdb2pqr with PROPKA at the run's pH. The two often place histidine hydrogens differently.

Checked against the protein structures in the PDB solved by neutron diffraction, where the hydrogens are placed from the data, reduce matched the deposited histidines more often than pdb2pqr (55% against 31% of 1,457 histidines) and turned fewer rings, and fewer Asn and Gln amides, the wrong way.

What reduce does not do is pH. It never protonates an Asp or Glu and rarely puts hydrogens on both nitrogens of a histidine, whatever the pH, while PROPKA chooses charges at the pH you set.

Prepare the receptor here, whichever you look at it with. Use ADapt to see what the file holds and decide what to keep, then let this interface prepare the receptor, so the docking and the rescoring use the same hydrogens. Then look at the histidines at your site and judge them.

What to do about it

  1. Set the pH your experiment runs at. It is the only control, and it is the one that matters.
  2. Read the prepared file at the histidines in your site, and note which nitrogen is free.
  3. Check the poses there. If the best poses all reach the protonated nitrogen, the result depends on a prediction rather than on a measurement.
  4. Run the other pH as a second run when the site has a histidine that PROPKA puts near its pKa. Two runs that agree are worth far more than one that cannot be questioned, and the pH is recorded in each run's settings.
