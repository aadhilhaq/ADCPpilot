Reading the results

One row per peptide, ranked, with the poses beside it. The table has more columns than it first shows, and several of them come with a limit on how far they can be pushed.

What the ranking is

Peptides are ranked most negative first, on the mean of the top few poses: the MM-GBSA mean where the rescoring ran, and ADCP's own mean where it did not. The column that decided it is named in the table's heading and in the first line of results.txt.

The mean rather than the best, because a best score is the tail of a distribution the search drew many times. Both are there, with the median and the spread.

Neither number is a binding free energy. Both are ranking aids within one run against one receptor. A peptide that ranks first here is the one to look at next, not the one to quote an affinity for.

The groups of columns

docking carries the length, how many poses ADCP ranked, the best score and the mean of the top few. A best score improves simply by scoring more poses, so read it beside the pose count. cluster population is how many of ADCP's clustered solutions landed in the top pose's cluster, and their share of all it clustered: ADCP returns one pose per cluster, so this is the search's own measure of its convergence. It lists at most a hundred clusters, so the share is an upper bound.

MM-GBSA is the best, the mean, the median, how many poses were rescored and their spread, the most positive minus the most negative among them. The spread says how much the poses disagree; it is not a test of the ranking.

MM-GBSA breakdown is the four parts of one pose's energy: van der Waals, electrostatic, polar solvation and nonpolar solvation. The pose is named in the group's first column: the lowest energy one among those measured.

nominated site is how many of the residues you named the pose touches, and their own share of the energy from the per residue breakdown. A pose can score well and touch almost none of your site, which is what this column exists to show.

interface is measured geometry: buried surface area and area per residue, the share of the peptide's surface the receptor covers, how much of the buried area is apolar, and counts of hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, stacked aromatics, contacts and clashes.

sequence is the peptide itself: mean hydropathy, net charge at the run's pH, the longest run of apolar residues, and a penalty total with its flags. It is sequence liability only, and not independent of the columns above, since the same sequence decides both.

how it was measured is off by default and worth turning on before you quote anything: how many poses were measured, over which atoms, on which coordinates, and how their minimisation ended.

The limits on these columns

  • Site coverage is quantised by the size of the site. On four residues it can only be 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, and residues the box could not reach still count in the denominator.
  • Contacts are geometric. A hydrogen bond here is a nitrogen or oxygen pair within 3.5 Å, with no angle tested; a stack is ring atoms within 5.0 Å, so an edge to face pair and a parallel stack are not told apart.
  • The per residue breakdown does not sum to the reported energy. It agrees to within a few per cent and is always slightly more negative, because a decomposition uses a per atom surface term where the total uses LCPO. The energy terms themselves agree to within 0.05 kcal/mol. The site dG column, in the page and in results.txt, is the site residues' share of that breakdown, so it carries the same small gap. The value for every residue is in the run's metrics/ folder.
  • Buried area over two different sets of atoms is not comparable. A rescored complex has hydrogens and a raw docked pose does not, and the same pose measured both ways differs by about 8 per cent. The column says which was used.
  • Clashes should be zero on a minimised complex. The receptor is restrained, so an overlap cannot relax out. Any clash is a reason to look at the pose.
  • The engines are on different scales. Read each one down its own column, never across a row.

The poses

view on a row opens that peptide's poses. The pose chooser steps through every pose kept, so you can see whether they agree on where the peptide binds or settle in several places.

Contacts are drawn as dashed lines with the measured heavy atom distance on each: a dashed line says two residues touch, and the number says how closely. Under the structure they are counted by type, hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, ring stacking and van der Waals, and each type can be switched off. Below that, the interaction diagram runs the peptide along the middle with each receptor residue over the one it is closest to, and it can be saved as an image. Per residue energies appear in it once the interface columns have been measured for that run.

The receptor's surface is drawn from the start, see-through, so the groove shows and the peptide stays in view inside it; rec steps it through mesh, off, solid and see-through, and pep does the same for the peptide's. Both are computed by MSMS from the receptor that was docked into and from the pose, with a 1.5 Å probe, as in the site view. Where MSMS gives none, the viewer draws its own and a line under the view says so.

figure switches to a view made for a paper: a pale ribbon, the contacting residues and the peptide as sticks, bonds as black dashes and labels as plain text. Labels can be dragged clear, and the leader lines are drawn into the image save image writes. Two viewers are bundled, and a figure from either needs its own citation.

The files the run writes

FileWhat it holds
results.tsvthe table, for a spreadsheet
results.jsonthe same rows, plus the constants every measured number was computed with
results.txtthe printable table, with its cautions about the data underneath
metrics/one file per peptide with every pose measured, and the residue map
docking/the ranked poses, as ADCP wrote them
HOW_TO_CITE.txt and two reference fileswhat to cite, scoped to this run

results.json carries the constants on purpose: a probe radius, a contact cutoff and a clash fraction are what let a number here be compared with one from elsewhere.

Every one of these files can be opened from the page itself: the Folders view of the Runs card walks the run's folder, draws a structure, shows a log or a table as text, and downloads the rest.

A run docked before version 0.1.17 has its energy terms but no interface columns, because the panel is measured while each pose is rescored. Its page says so and offers to measure them from the files the MM-GBSA already left.
