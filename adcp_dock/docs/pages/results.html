Reading the results

One row per peptide, ranked, with the poses beside it. The table has more columns than it first shows, and several of them come with a limit on how far they can be pushed.

What the ranking is

Peptides are ranked most negative first, on the mean of the top few poses: the MM-GBSA mean where the rescoring ran, and ADCP's own mean where it did not. The column that decided it is named in the table's heading and in the first line of results.txt.

The mean rather than the best, because a best score is the tail of a distribution the search drew many times. Both are there, with the median and the spread.

Neither number is a binding free energy. Both are ranking aids within one run against one receptor. A peptide that ranks first here is the one to look at next, not the one to quote an affinity for.

To resize a column, drag the thin grip on the right edge of its heading, or tab to the grip and use the left and right arrow keys; double click it to fit the column to its widest entry. The pinned columns on the left stay pinned, and a value too long for its column ends in an ellipsis, with the whole of it on hover. The widths are remembered in this browser, also for the residue tables under the pose viewer, and Reset column widths under a table puts them back.

The groups of columns

docking carries the length, how many poses ADCP ranked, the best score and the mean of the top few. When any peptide in the run was docked as a ring, it carries a ring column as well: linear, head to tail or disulfide, for each peptide as it was docked. A best score improves simply by scoring more poses, so read it beside the pose count. cluster population is how many of ADCP's clustered solutions landed in the top pose's cluster, and their share of all it clustered: ADCP returns one pose per cluster, so this is the search's own measure of its convergence. It lists at most a hundred clusters, so the share is an upper bound.

MM-GBSA is the best, the mean, the median, how many poses were rescored and their spread, the most positive minus the most negative among them. The spread says how much the poses disagree; it is not a test of the ranking. A ring is rescored only on the poses that close it, so its count can be smaller than the others'. A peptide with no value at all is listed at the end of results.txt as unranked, with the reason beside its name.

Peptide strain, after the MM-GBSA columns, is what the peptide pays to hold its docked shape, for the pose the row's other per pose numbers describe. It is never ranked on; it has a section of its own below.

MM-GBSA breakdown is the four parts of one pose's energy: van der Waals, electrostatic, polar solvation and nonpolar solvation. The pose is named in the group's first column: the lowest energy one among those measured.

nominated site is how many of the residues you named the pose touches, and their own share of the energy from the per residue breakdown. A pose can score well and touch almost none of your site, which is what this column exists to show.

interface is measured geometry: buried surface area and area per residue, the share of the peptide's surface the receptor covers, how much of the buried area is apolar, and counts of hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, stacked aromatics, contacts and clashes.

sequence is the peptide itself: mean hydropathy, net charge at the run's pH, the longest run of apolar residues, and a penalty total with its flags. It is sequence liability only, and not independent of the columns above, since the same sequence decides both.

how it was measured is off by default and worth turning on before you quote anything: how many poses were measured, over which atoms, on which coordinates, and how their minimization ended.

The limits on these columns

  • Site coverage is quantized by the size of the site. On four residues it can only be 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, and residues the box could not reach still count in the denominator.
  • Contacts are geometric. A hydrogen bond here is a nitrogen or oxygen pair within 3.5 Å, with no angle tested; a stack is ring atoms within 5.0 Å, so an edge to face pair and a parallel stack are not told apart.
  • The per residue breakdown does not sum to the reported energy. It agrees to within a few percent and is always slightly more negative, because a decomposition uses a per atom surface term where the total uses LCPO. The energy terms themselves agree to within 0.05 kcal/mol. The site dG column, in the page and in results.txt, is the site residues' share of that breakdown, so it carries the same small gap. The value for every residue is in the run's metrics/ folder.
  • Buried area over two different sets of atoms is not comparable. A rescored complex has hydrogens and a raw docked pose does not, and the same pose measured both ways differs by about 8 percent. The column says which was used.
  • Clashes should be zero on a minimized complex. The receptor is restrained, so an overlap cannot relax out. Any clash is a reason to look at the pose.
  • The engines are on different scales. Read each one down its own column, never across a row.

The peptide strain

Peptide strain is what the peptide pays to hold its docked shape: its energy in the pose, minus the same peptide relaxed on its own from that shape, in kcal/mol, in implicit solvent with the run's own GB model. Lower is easier. The column shows the strain of the pose the row's other per pose numbers describe, the one with the lowest MM-GBSA energy, and it is not part of the MM-GBSA ranking or of the combined score.

The peptide is measured alone, with the receptor taken away: once in the shape it was scored in, and once relaxed from that shape by 5,000 steps of unrestrained conjugate gradient in AmberTools' sander, with the generalized Born model, the salt and the surface term of the run's own MM-GBSA. The step count is fixed, so the same pose always gives the same number; the relaxed peptide is not the bottom of its well. Each pose takes about 30 to 45 seconds, several at once.

  • It grows with length. A free peptide folds onto itself in implicit solvent, so a longer one relaxes further: on the 120 poses the method was settled on, the medians ran from 17 to 26 kcal/mol between 6 and 12 residues. Compare peptides of similar length.
  • A very large strain is a pose to look at. A docked shape with distorted bond angles shows as a strain of more than a hundred kcal/mol, which is what the column is for.
  • A cell can be empty. A pose whose strain could not be measured shows no number, never a 0, and the reason is under the pointer.
  • A run scored by Schrödinger Prime shows Prime's own ligand strain, read from what Prime wrote, on its own force field and solvent model.

A run made now measures it right after each pose's MM-GBSA. A finished run that has none gets it from Add measures, from the files its MM-GBSA left, without docking or rescoring anything again.

The poses

view on a row opens that peptide's poses. The pose chooser steps through every pose kept, so you can see whether they agree on where the peptide binds or settle in several places.

Contacts are drawn as dashed lines with the measured heavy atom distance on each: a dashed line says two residues touch, and the number says how closely. Under the structure they are counted by type, hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, ring stacking and van der Waals, and each type can be switched off. Below that, the interaction diagram runs the peptide along the middle with each receptor residue over the one it is closest to, and it can be saved as an image. Per residue energies appear in it once the interface columns have been measured for that run.

The receptor's surface is drawn from the start, see-through, so the groove shows and the peptide stays in view inside it; rec steps it through mesh, off, solid and see-through, and pep does the same for the peptide's. Both are computed by MSMS from the receptor that was docked into and from the pose, with a 1.5 Å probe, as in the site view. Where MSMS gives none, the viewer draws its own and a line under the view says so.

figure switches to a view made for a paper: a pale ribbon, the contacting residues and the peptide as sticks, bonds as black dashes and labels as plain text. Labels can be dragged clear, and the leader lines are drawn into the image save image writes. Two viewers are bundled, and a figure from either needs its own citation.

The report

Report (PDF), under a run's table, opens the run written up in a new tab, ready to save as a PDF. It reads like the results section of a paper: a summary of which peptide ranks first and whether the run separates it from the next ones; the receptor's preparation and the site and box; the peptides ranked, with a chart and the interface panel; then each peptide on a page of its own, with its poses and their scores, pictures of the top poses seen side on, the interaction diagram of the pose the panel describes and its residues one by one, its ring and its sequence flags. The views added from the viewer follow, then a methods section written from what the run did, with its references, and an appendix with the preparation log, AGFR's log and every setting.

Every caption states the result from the run's own numbers, then only what is needed to read it: "HOPE4_3 ranks first by MM-GBSA, −69.0 ± 14.4 kcal/mol, 5.9 ahead of HOPE9_1", not a title. A run docked with ADCP 1.0, before this tool moved to ADCP 1.1 or on a Mac, says so above everything else, since its scores are on another scale.

The bar along the top chooses how many peptides get a page, how many poses are drawn, which poses get a diagram and whether van der Waals contacts are drawn in it, and which sections are included. Save as PDF opens the browser's print window, where Save as PDF is the printer; text and diagrams stay vector, and tables and figures are kept whole on a page. Copy the methods and references puts them on the clipboard to paste into a paper. To read the page on screen, + and − zoom it from 50% to 200%, fit width makes it as wide as the window and the percentage itself goes back to 100%; the zoom is kept in this browser and never reaches the PDF.

add to report, on the viewer's bar, adds the view exactly as it is, in 3Dmol or Mol*, with that pose's interaction diagram. Write your own words under it in the report itself. Views are kept with the run, in its report/ folder, and can be moved or taken out from the report.

The files the run writes

FileWhat it holds
results.tsvthe table, for a spreadsheet
results.jsonthe same rows, plus the constants every measured number was computed with
results.txtthe printable table, with its cautions about the data underneath
metrics/one file per peptide with every pose measured, and the residue map
docking/the ranked poses, as ADCP wrote them
HOW_TO_CITE.txt and two reference fileswhat to cite, scoped to this run
report/the views added to the report from the viewer, with your words for them
archive/once the run is tidied, the files its results no longer read, in one archive

results.json carries the constants on purpose: a probe radius, a contact cutoff and a clash fraction are what let a number here be compared with one from elsewhere.

Every one of these files can be opened from the page itself: the Folders view of the Runs card walks the run's folder, draws a structure, shows a log or a table as text, and downloads the rest.

A run docked before version 0.1.17 has its energy terms but no interface columns, because the panel is measured while each pose is rescored, and a run rescored before the peptide strain existed has no strain. Its page says so in one line over its table, and Add them… there measures what it lacks from the files the MM-GBSA already left; Add measures does the same for many runs at once.
