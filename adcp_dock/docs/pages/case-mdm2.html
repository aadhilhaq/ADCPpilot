MDM2 and the p53 peptide

The run that ships with the package, end to end: three known binders docked into the p53 groove of MDM2, and what their table does and does not tell you.

The target

PDB entry 1YCR is MDM2 with the p53 transactivation peptide bound. Chain A is MDM2, chain B is the peptide. For docking, chain A is the receptor and chain B is dropped, because it is exactly what the peptides are competing for. The structure is a good first target: the site is a well defined hydrophobic groove, and three peptides with different sequences are known to bind it.

Both measures put the three in the same order, which is worth more than either alone. The four parts of p53's best pose energy are van der Waals -92.5, electrostatic -374.8, polar solvation +405.0 and nonpolar solvation -12.2 kcal/mol: a large electrostatic attraction almost cancelled by the cost of desolvating it, with the van der Waals term carrying the interaction. That is the ordinary shape of a hydrophobic groove.

What to notice before quoting it

The spread is as large as the differences. p53's five rescored poses span 27.0 kcal/mol, and p53 and PMI are 11.7 apart. The noise within one peptide is a large share of the signal between peptides. That is a property of the docked poses, not of the rescoring, and no choice of engine removes it.

Three more things this run shows honestly:

  • The search was small. 25 replicas by 2,000,000 steps is below every published setting, because the run ships inside a Python package. The guideline is 100 to 300 replicas and 1 to 3 million steps per residue, so a 13-mer wants 13 to 39 million. Do not take this run's settings as a template.
  • The top cluster is thin. p53's best pose comes from a cluster of 68 of the solutions ADCP clustered, which the table shows as 6 per cent of them, across 100 modes. A converged search puts much more of its weight in one place.
  • Three site residues fall outside the box. Residues 25, 70 and 26 miss it by 2.7, 1.3 and 0.6 Å. No pose can reach them, and they still count in the site coverage denominator. Naming fewer, better chosen residues would have given a tighter box and a cleaner coverage number.

The example also carries no interface columns. Its intermediate geometry was not kept, so only the energy terms could be read back, and results.txt says so in as many words. A run of your own, kept whole, has the buried area, the contacts and the site coverage as well.

Repeating it properly

  1. Open the example and press reuse settings. The receptor, the chain, the 22 residues, the box and the three peptides come back into the form.
  2. Narrow the site to the residues a binder must touch, so the box stops being clamped, and check the cage.
  3. Raise the search to 100 replicas and about 13,000,000 steps for the 13-mer.
  4. Press Estimate & fill, give it a new name, and submit.

Then compare the two tables. If the order holds and the spread narrows, the ranking is telling you something about the peptides. If the order moves, it was telling you about the search.
